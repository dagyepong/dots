Field notes on walking over words

A short essay · 6 min read

Every page is a landscape of small words. We cross it so quickly that we rarely notice the terrain: the long flat stretches of a paragraph, the sudden steps of a heading, the bright little bridges of links that lead somewhere else. A careful walker would notice all of it.

Imagine something that reads with its feet. It tests each line before it trusts it, places one foot on a noun, another on a verb, and leans its weight across a comma. Where a foot lands, the word wakes up for a moment: it glows, it shifts, it echoes, and then it settles back exactly as it was.

Terrain

Short words are pebbles. Long words are logs you can stand on with two feet at once. Numbers are surprisingly firm footing, and quotation marks make for slippery edges. A list is a staircase; a table is a field of stepping stones laid out in rows.

Nothing on the page is changed. The walker only reads, and the words remember being read.

Margins are open ground. Nobody lives there, and a walker crossing them moves faster, legs reaching for the next solid word on the far side.

Silk

Behind the walker, thin bright threads trail from word to word, like notes in the margin that only last a second. Some are pulled taut and stretch a whole phrase into a ribbon of colour. Others snap back almost before you see them.

  • Threads reach for words a little too far away to step on.
  • Ribbons form when a foot holds on while the body moves on.
  • Echoes are the words that were read most recently, drifting off to the side.

Reading speed

Left alone, the walker reads the way most of us do: steadily downward, with the occasional pause to look around. Given a cursor to follow, it becomes curious and chases it across the page. Given a shortcut, it appears and disappears on command.

There is no destination. The point is the walk itself, and the small, brief changes it leaves in the text: a word outlined in blue, another lit up like a highlighter stroke, a third suddenly set in a typewriter face twice its usual size.

Afterwards

When the walk is over, the page is exactly what it was before. Every word is back in its place. Reading, it turns out, leaves no marks on the text, only on the reader.

Still, it is nice to imagine that somewhere between one line and the next, a small, careful creature is testing the words before it trusts them, and finding most of them sound.
